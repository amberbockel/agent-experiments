# Deep End — director's treatment

*Working title. Private. Draft 1, after studies 01–04.*

## Logline

A girl in sunglasses stands on a lawn in 1989. Behind her is a house made of every photograph she will ever keep. You fall through it. Some of the photographs have openings. Everything you pick up on the way opens something at the bottom.

## The rule that governs everything

Every door is an opening that already exists in one of her photographs. The tube her son crawled out of. The hotel doorway she stood in on her wedding day. The letter H. A cobbled alley in Europe. Nothing is drawn that could be cut from a real picture instead. When the material needs something she never photographed, it comes from the public archive, and it is always a century old: Tenniel, Haeckel, Muybridge, Grandville, Audubon.

## Structure

**Prologue — the lawn (floor 1).** Curtains open. She is singing. The White Rabbit runs past in five copies. Birds cross. The visitor learns three things without being told: you fall if you do nothing, the lamp follows your hand, paper rustles when you touch it.

**The hall of four doors (bottom of floor 1).** Four photographs side by side, each with a live view through its opening. Labels in her handwriting: *love*, *travel*, *motherhood*, *adventure*. This is the first choice and it is the whole film in miniature: four lives, one body.

**The wings.** Each door opens a sequence of three rooms. Every room has exactly two ways out: *down*, which drops you back into the column deeper than you were, and *sideways*, which goes further into the wing. Sideways is slower and that is where the things are.

| wing | room 1 | room 2 | room 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| love | the street, the red shoes | the dance, the bench under the bridge | later: the gala, the beanie, the young ones |
| travel | Zürich, the swans, the lion | the museum, the balloon dog, the Murakami wall | the coast, the palm, the hammock, and the red car |
| motherhood | the hospital, the grandparents | the kitchen, cooking with him on a hip | held: every photograph of someone holding someone |
| adventure | the wind tunnel, the jump, the zipline | horses, hers and his, and Muybridge's | the night: the fire, the lanterns, the jeep |

**The drive.** In the coast room the red Mercedes is parked. Click it and you are on the road. It is not a driving game. It is a toy car pushed through a diorama: a paper road scrolling toward you, hoodoos and palms and prints of old photographs sweeping past on sticks, birds overhead, the synth doubling to an arpeggio. Twenty-six seconds. It ends at Bryce Canyon at dusk with William standing at the rim, because that is where the real road went.

**The column (floors 2–5).** The carousel, the water, the yellow room, the dark. Each floor has one small door of its own, so the wings are not the only way sideways.

**The mirror (floor 5).** Her son's gilded mirror, glass punched out. It opens only for stillness, and only if the pocket holds the rabbit. Behind it is the first photograph: her, held, before any of this.

**The reveal (not yet built).** After the mirror, the camera keeps pulling back. The whole column was standing inside the girl in sunglasses. She has been on the lawn the entire time. The visitor fell through her.

## Recurring characters

- **The girl in sunglasses.** The guide. She sings on the lawn and she is the house.
- **The White Rabbit.** Tenniel's, borrowed. He appears on every floor and sits still only once, in the rocks. That is when you can take him.
- **The birds.** Paper silhouettes in flocks of seven, flapping in time. They cross every floor and the road. They are the only thing in the house that never stops moving.
- **The red car.** It appears three times: parked on the lawn as a small thing, parked in the coast room as a door, and at the bottom of the dark, driving away.
- **The hand.** Vesalius's. It points. It is the house's only signage.

## Sound

- A house groove, four on the floor, synthesized in the browser, so the house always has a pulse. A dropped song replaces it and T taps the tempo.
- Every floor filters the groove differently: bright on the lawn, submerged in the water, hollow in the dark.
- The drive doubles the pulse into a square-wave arpeggio.
- Rooms are quieter than the column. Entering one pulls the low end out.
- Paper rustles on hover. Doors swell. Items click.
- No licensed audio ships. Songs that raised the house appear as paper: sleeves, labels, a lyric on the back of a print.

## What the visitor is actually doing

Choosing between falling and wandering. The column is the fast path and arrives with nothing. The wings are slow and arrive with the thing that opens the end. Nobody is told this. The pocket shows it.

## Open decisions for the director

1. Proscenium: keep or cut.
2. Whether the hall of doors is at the bottom of the lawn or replaces the lawn entirely.
3. Whether the wings can connect to each other (love 3 opens into travel 1) or stay separate.
4. The ending: the reveal as written, or something quieter.
